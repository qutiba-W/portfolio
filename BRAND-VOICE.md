## Voice Summary
A working engineer's voice, not a marketer's: it earns trust with measured
numbers instead of adjectives, explains *why* a decision was made instead
of just stating it, and undersells rather than oversells (see: the site's
own honesty note about status badges). It reads as calm, precise, and
quietly confident — never hyped, never apologetic.

## Voice Dimensions

### Formal <-----> Casual: 4/10
Leans professional but not stiff. Full sentences and technical register
("Structural instrumentation, analog circuit design, parametric CAD, and
CNC laser fabrication") sit next to plain, contraction-using lines ("I
build things to finish them," "Don't stand over it," "it isn't a
walk-past"). No slang, no greetings, always first person.

### Serious <-----> Playful: 3/10
Not stern, but not playful either — closer to *dry and understated* than
either extreme. No emoji, no exclamation marks anywhere in the analyzed
copy, no puns. The closest thing to levity is a clipped, almost deadpan
aphorism at the end of a section: "Heat is a symptom, not the problem."
"This rig closes the gap." "Demount, inspect, then decide."

### Technical <-----> Simple: 4/10
Genuinely technical — real units, real precision (`~1.5 g`, `5.83 Hz`,
`I ≈ 2.15×10⁻¹² m⁴`, `E = 200 GPa`, `95% accuracy`) — but never opaque.
Every technical claim is immediately followed by the reasoning behind it
("Steel was chosen for its known elastic behaviour and a clean,
calculable moment of inertia"). It assumes an engineering-literate reader
but never assumes you already know the specific system.

### Reserved <-----> Bold: 4/10
Confident but not boastful. The tagline ("I build things that work.") is
bold in its plainness, not in bravado — there's no "revolutionary,"
"world-class," or "passionate" anywhere in the copy. The site explicitly
states its own honesty as a value: *"Status badges are honest: Live means
deployed and clickable, Local means built but not hosted yet."* That's a
voice that would rather underclaim than overclaim.

## Brand Personality
- **Primary Archetype: The Guide** — every case study is structured as a
  teaching walkthrough (The Problem / The Setup / What We Covered /
  Architecture), always explaining the reasoning, never just the result.
- **Secondary Archetype: The Authority** — backed by real measured data
  rather than credentials-flexing; earns authority through evidence, not
  claims.
- **Archetype fit: Strong.** The one deliberate deviation from a pure
  Authority voice is humility appropriate to being a student, not a
  20-year veteran — claims are always scoped to what was actually built
  and measured, never generalized into industry pronouncements.

## Vocabulary

### Words We Use
**Action words:** build, ship, instrument, extract, recover, document, deliver, close (the gap)

**Descriptive words:** real, honest, real-world, structured, documented, deployed, offline-first, lightweight

**Value words:** honest, documented, real (used almost as a value statement — "real-world datasets," "real time")

**Domain terms used without over-explaining:** strain gauge, FFT, PWA, service worker, moment of inertia, Random Forest, kerf-compensated

### Words We Avoid
- Hype adjectives: "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "world-class," "game-changing," "passionate," "synergy"
- Vague superlatives without a number behind them ("the best," "amazing")
- Exclamation marks
- Emoji
- Passive corporate hedging ("it is believed that," "efforts were made to")

### Signature Phrases / Patterns
- No fixed tagline beyond "I build things that work." — but a recurring
  *rhythm*: one plain declarative sentence, then an em-dash, then the
  specific proof point. ("Brain training PWA with six games and offline
  support — runs in the browser with no install required.")
- Case-study sections almost always end on a short, clipped, standalone
  sentence that functions like a rule of thumb: "Demount, inspect, then
  decide." "Heat is a symptom, not the problem."
- Numbers are always specific, never rounded for effect: `5.83 Hz`, not
  "about 6 Hz"; `95% accuracy`, not "highly accurate."

## Writing Guidelines

### Do's
- Back every claim with a real, specific number when one exists (a
  measurement, a percentage, a count) — never round it for rhythm.
- Explain the *why* behind a technical decision in the same breath as the
  decision itself.
- End a section on a short, standalone sentence that reads like a
  takeaway, not a summary restatement.
- Use the em-dash rhythm: plain statement, dash, specific proof.
- State limitations and status plainly ("built but not hosted yet")
  rather than smoothing them over.

### Don'ts
- Don't use exclamation marks or emoji anywhere in site copy.
- Don't reach for hype adjectives ("revolutionary," "world-class") — if a
  project is good, a specific number should be doing that work instead.
- Don't round a measured number for cleaner prose.
- Don't oversell project status — "Local" and "Archive" badges exist
  specifically so the copy never has to.
- Don't write a generic "passionate about X" line — every claim on this
  site is scoped to something actually built.

## Competitor Voice Comparison

No specific competitor sites were provided or scraped for this analysis.
For a personal engineering portfolio, the more useful reference point is
against two common *portfolio archetypes* rather than named rivals:

| Dimension | This Portfolio | Typical "hype" student portfolio | Typical corporate/agency portfolio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal <> Casual | 4/10 | 7/10 (very casual, first-person "I'm passionate about...") | 2/10 (formal, third-person) |
| Serious <> Playful | 3/10 | 6/10 (emoji, exclamation marks common) | 2/10 |
| Technical <> Simple | 4/10 (technical but explained) | 7/10 (oversimplified, "cool project!") | 5/10 (buzzword-technical, under-explained) |
| Reserved <> Bold | 4/10 | 8/10 (heavy self-promotion, no evidence) | 6/10 (confident claims, client-logo-driven) |
| Primary Archetype | The Guide | The Rebel (trying to stand out via personality) | The Authority (trying to stand out via scale/credentials) |

**Differentiation Assessment:** The unoccupied territory here is exactly
where this site already sits — evidence-driven confidence without hype.
Most student portfolios oversell with adjectives because they don't have
measured results to point to; this one has them (1.5 g, 5.83 Hz, 95%) and
uses them instead of adjectives. The main risk is not divergence but
*dilution* — any new copy (marketing pages, category intros, About
rewrites) that leans on adjectives instead of numbers would quietly erode
the thing that currently makes this voice distinct.

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions
1. Use this doc as the reference for the upcoming SEO and copy passes —
   any new headline, meta description, or CTA should pass the "would a
   number do this job better than an adjective?" test before shipping.
2. When writing new meta descriptions/SEO copy, keep the existing
   quantified-claim pattern (e.g., "95% accuracy," "5.83 Hz") rather than
   switching to generic SEO phrasing ("innovative solutions," "cutting-edge").

### Voice Evolution Opportunities
1. The dry, aphoristic section-closers ("Heat is a symptom, not the
   problem") are the most distinctive and least-used device on the site —
   there's room to use one more deliberately in category-page intros,
   which are currently the flattest copy on the site (single scoped
   sentence, no closer).

### Consistency Improvements
1. None needed structurally — the main thing to guard against is new
   copy (from SEO/marketing work) introducing hype language that doesn't
   match the existing, evidence-first tone.
