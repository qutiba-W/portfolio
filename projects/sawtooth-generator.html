Sawtooth Wave Generator

Analog op-amp circuit that generates asymmetric sawtooth waveforms using a Schmitt trigger driving an integrator through a diode RC network. Built and tested on breadboard.

The Problem

A symmetric triangle wave is the textbook output of a Schmitt-trigger oscillator. A sawtooth needs something extra: the integrator has to charge slowly and discharge fast. This circuit gets that asymmetry purely with analog components — no microcontroller, no function-generator IC.

How It Works

Schmitt trigger — the timing brain

A comparator with hysteresis flips its output when the integrator's ramp crosses the switching thresholds at ±2.04 V. Its square-wave output tells the integrator which direction to ramp.

Integrator — the ramp

An op-amp integrator built around 100 kΩ / 100 nF converts the square wave into a linear ramp between the trigger thresholds.

Diode RC network — the asymmetry

Steering diodes route the integrator current through different resistances on the rising and falling half-cycles, skewing the ramp rates by a factor of k ≈ 17.9 — a slow rise and a near-vertical fall: a sawtooth.

Build & Test

The full circuit runs from a split ±12 V supply and was assembled and verified on breadboard, with the output waveform and switching thresholds checked against the design calculations on an oscilloscope.
