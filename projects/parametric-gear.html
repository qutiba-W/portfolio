Parametric Gear System

Print-in-place gear train with 0.2 mm clearance — designed for FDM PLA, breaks free and rotates immediately off the build plate without post-processing.

The Objective

Designing for additive manufacturing (DFAM) requires accounting for phenomena that don't exist in subtractive machining: thermal expansion of deposited layers, resolution limits of FDM, and the risk of adjacent surfaces fusing during printing. A print-in-place mechanism must have gaps tight enough to hold form but wide enough that heat from deposition doesn't bond the moving parts together.

Technical Details

0.2 mm tolerance engineering

Gear tooth profiles were designed with 0.2 mm radial clearance on each face — enough to prevent PLA fusion at 210°C deposition temperature while still keeping the gears co-planar and meshed after cooling.

Interference detection before slicing

SolidWorks' interference detection was run on the full assembly before export. Any mesh overlap would fuse in print; the model was iterated until zero interference at the design tolerance, then confirmed in Cura's layer preview before printing.

Parametric tooth profiles

Tooth geometry is driven by module and pressure angle variables. Changing the module updates all gear profiles consistently — the print-in-place clearance scales with tooth size automatically.
